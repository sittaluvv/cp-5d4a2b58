About

Abdul El-Sayed

The son of Egyptian immigrants, born and raised in southeast Michigan, Abdul El-Sayed is the first Muslim American elected to the United States Senate.[4][5]

A public health leader by training, he graduated from the University of Michigan, earned an Oxford doctorate in public health as a Rhodes Scholar, and received his medical degree from Columbia.[5]

  • DetroitExecutive Director of the Detroit Health Department, 2015–2017.[5]
  • Wayne CountyDirector of Health, Human & Veterans Services, 2023–2025. A county program had erased about $95 million in medical debt for more than 100,000 residents as of September 2026.[5]
  • Habibi PACRegistered with the FEC as a leadership PAC sponsored by El-Sayed, November 5–6, 2026.[1][2]

Mission

Building power beyond a single seat.

Habibi PAC is a leadership PAC: a committee separate from Abdul El-Sayed’s own campaign, meant to raise money for his political allies.[3][4]

I.

Back allies

Leadership PACs are committees overseen by politicians that can raise money and spend on colleagues or preferred candidates.[9]

II.

Strengthen harder races

Leadership PAC dollars typically go to allies and “politicians who have a lower profile and a harder time raising money or are in some of the most competitive elections.”[9]

III.

Transform politics

“Winning a single U.S. Senate seat was never the goal. The goal is to use that seat to transform our politics.” — Abdul El-Sayed[10]

Why Habibi

A word of affection, answered to division.

Habibi is an Arabic term of endearment meaning “my beloved.”[3] El-Sayed used it often on the campaign trail, and supporters wore “Habibi for Abdul” shirts.[3][4]

After what he called “one of the ugliest campaigns we’ve seen in our lifetimes,” he answered with a term of endearment, and now with a committee to help elect allies.[3][10]

“This work is bigger than one senator. It’s about changing the face of our politics.”
Abdul El-Sayed, victory speech[10]
